How a heap fits in an array

A heap has two separate rules: its shape is a complete binary tree (fill each level from left to right), and its order puts each parent before its children. A min-heap has a parent no larger than its children; a max-heap has a parent no smaller than its children.

Store the tree level by level from left to right. The root is at index 0. For a node at index k, its parent is floor((k - 1) / 2); its children are at 2k + 1 and 2k + 2. These formulas work because a complete tree has no gaps in its levels.

Example: index 1 is the left child of index 0, and index 2 is the right child. Their children are at indices 3, 4, 5, and 6.
